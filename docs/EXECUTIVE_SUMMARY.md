# UMA — Executive Summary
## Hyperlocal B2B Agricultural Marketplace & Pooled Logistics Platform
**Location**: Butuan City, Agusan del Norte (Caraga Region, Philippines)  
**Target Focus**: Smallholder vegetable farmers → Institutional business kitchens (Carinderias, Canteens, Restaurants, Resorts)

---

### 1. The Core Problem
Produce procurement in Butuan is fragmented, informal, and dominated by traditional *bagsakan* middlemen who extract **20% to 30%+ margins** while offering poor visibility on both ends:
- **Smallholder Farmers**: Face unpredictable buyer demand, lack direct transport to city commercial hubs, and absorb heavy price discounting from middlemen.
- **Institutional Food Businesses**: Suffer erratic supply, inconsistent grading, fluctuating daily prices, and the hassle of coordinating with dozens of individual vendors.

Direct transactions fail because farmers cannot justify the delivery cost for small baskets, and businesses cannot afford to manage separate logistics for every crop.

---

### 2. The UMA Solution
UMA is an integrated digital marketplace and logistics layer (**UMA Courier**) engineered specifically for **recurring B2B agricultural procurement**:

```
[Smallholder Farmers] ──(Forward Listings)──► [UMA Platform] ◄──(Scheduled Basket Orders)── [Commercial Buyers]
                                                    │
                                     [UMA Courier (Pooled Logistics)]
                                                    │
                               [Consolidated Delivery & Escrow Settlement]
```

* **Forward-Order (Harvest-to-Order) Model**: Farmers list expected yields against a specific harvest date. Crops are harvested **only after orders are confirmed**, eliminating field waste and unsold market inventory.
* **Many-to-Many Pooled Logistics**: Rather than one-to-one on-demand dispatch (Grab/Foodpanda model), UMA batches multiple farm pickups and consolidates deliveries along high-density barangay corridors.
* **Basket Aggregation**: A restaurant places a single order containing tomatoes, eggplant, and squash from 3 different farmers, delivered in a single shipment at a scheduled time.

---

### 3. What UMA Is NOT
* ❌ **Not Foodpanda/Grab for produce**: Not on-demand single-drop consumer delivery.
* ❌ **Not a speculative spot-market**: No warehouse holding decaying produce.
* ❌ **Not claiming unprovable "100% Freshness"**: Freshness is enforced through **process controls** (harvest-to-dropoff time caps, mandatory pickup/dropoff photo timestamps, and pre-acceptance inspection).
* ❌ **Not an immediate national competitor to Mayani or AgriBOOST**: UMA wins through **hyperlocal operational density** in Caraga before expanding.

---

### 4. The Five-Layer Lock (Preventing Platform Disintermediation)
A common defense critique is: *"Once a farmer meets a buyer, they will transact via text to avoid fees."* UMA counters this through 5 structural moats:

1. **Logistics Dependency (Basket Aggregation)**: Going direct forces a carinderia to arrange 3–4 separate pickups instead of receiving one aggregated delivery.
2. **Working Capital Float (Phase 2)**: UMA pays farmers on delivery while offering 7–14 day trade credit to vetted institutional buyers—a float smallholder farmers cannot provide alone.
3. **Replacement & Quality Guarantees**: If a crop fails or is rejected, UMA automatically reroutes fulfillment to a backup farmer within the network.
4. **Network Intelligence & Transparent Pricing**: Buyers receive seasonal price and yield benchmarks across the entire regional network.
5. **Institutional Service Level Agreements (SLAs)**: High-volume resort and hotel buyers are anchored via structured supply agreements.

---

### 5. Unit Economics & Business Model (MVP Baseline)
UMA operates on a **volume-driven, low-margin model**. Transparency regarding thin margins proves operational credibility over inflated financial projections:

| Line Item | Example (Carinderia Order) | Operational Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gross Produce Value** | **₱2,000.00** | Standard 3–4 crop basket order |
| **Delivery Fee (Charged to Buyer)** | **₱150.00** | Flat one-to-one baseline delivery fee |
| **Total Charged to Buyer** | **₱2,150.00** | Simple, transparent checkout |
| **Commission (8% from Produce)** | **₱160.00** | Deducted from farmer payout (nets > bagsakan) |
| **Farmer Payout** | **₱1,840.00** | Paid upon delivery confirmation |
| **Courier Payout** | **₱100.00** | Partnered motorcycle/multicab courier rate |
| **Logistics Margin** | **₱50.00** | ₱150 delivery fee − ₱100 courier payout |
| **Payment Gateway (~2%)** | **₱43.00** | Transaction processing fee |
| **Support & Operations** | **₱30.00** | Platform overhead per transaction |
| **Spoilage / Refund Reserve (~3%)** | **₱60.00** | Reserve fund for transit damage/disputes |
| **UMA Net Contribution Margin** | **₱77.00 (~3.6%)** | **Volume and repeat frequency are essential** |

---

### 6. MVP Pilot Scope (Butuan City)
* **Geography**: 1 candidate farming cluster (e.g., La Paz / Buenavista) to Butuan City commercial center.
* **Pilot Cohort**: 5–10 verified vegetable farmers + 5–10 institutional kitchens (canteens/carinderias).
* **Delivery**: Partnered local couriers (no owned fleet at launch to avoid capital expenditure and labor classification liabilities).
* **Payment**: Pay-on-delivery (cash/digital transfer) to eliminate credit default risk during validation.
